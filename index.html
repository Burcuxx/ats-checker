What decides whether a recruiter finds your CV
Most companies screen applications with an ATS. Recruiters search and rank candidates by keyword. These four things decide whether your CV shows up.

      

        
1The file has to be readable
Scanned images, tables and multi-column designs cause text to get jumbled or lost.

        
2The CV uses the advert's language
Recruiters search using the terms in the ad. Describe the same skills with the same words.

        
3The structure is standard
Familiar headings like "Experience", "Education" and "Skills" help the ATS put your details in the right fields.

        
4Achievements are measurable
After the ATS, a human reads your CV. Results backed by numbers stand out.

      

    

  


  

    

      
Questions about the CV checker

      

        
Is the CV checker free?
Yes. No sign-up or payment needed.

        
Is my CV uploaded to a server?
No. Your file is processed entirely in your browser and is never sent anywhere.

        
What is an ATS?
An Applicant Tracking System is software companies use to collect applications, extract information from CVs and search for candidates. Workday, Greenhouse and Lever are common examples.

        
How is the score calculated?
Checks such as readability, layout, sections and contact details are scored by weight. Bullets are scored proportionally: if half your bullets have no number, you get half the points for that check. If you add a job ad, keyword match makes up 30% of the score. Scores differ between tools because each uses its own criteria.

        
Should I send a PDF or a Word file?
Both work in most ATS. What matters is that the PDF contains real text (not a scan) and uses a simple single-column layout.
